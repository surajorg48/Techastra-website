DEVICE-AS-A-SERVICE

Business Requirement Document + Go-To-Market Strategy

Managed Smartphone Rental for PropTech, Society Management & Field Operations

# 1. Executive Summary

Techastra will operate a Device-as-a-Service (DaaS) business that supplies smartphones on a recurring rental basis to technology companies whose software requires dedicated devices at customer locations. The initial anchor use case is M Square's residential-society and gated-community security/visitor-management software.

Under the proposed model, M Square acquires societies and deploys its software. Techastra supplies the required smartphones, retains device ownership, and manages the device lifecycle under an annual B2B contract. The initial commercial assumption is ₹200 per device per month, with approximately two devices required per society.

# 2. Business Vision

Build a scalable managed-device platform that removes the hardware procurement, deployment and lifecycle burden from software companies and their customers.

## Mission

Provide reliable, affordable and deployment-ready business devices on a predictable recurring-cost model.

## Positioning

Managed Device-as-a-Service for business software deployments.

# 3. Problem Statement

Software companies may need dedicated smartphones at customer sites but do not want to purchase and manage hardware.

Societies and gated communities may not want to procure, configure, replace and maintain security devices themselves.

Hardware procurement creates upfront capital expenditure, administrative work and device-lifecycle problems.

Lost, damaged or outdated devices can disrupt software adoption.

As software customer acquisition grows, device demand grows proportionally and requires a scalable supply partner.

# 4. Proposed Solution

Techastra becomes the hardware and device-lifecycle partner.

Procure suitable Android smartphones at controlled cost.

Configure and prepare devices for business deployment.

Supply devices against confirmed customer/society deployments.

Maintain device ownership during the rental term.

Manage replacement, repair and lifecycle processes according to contract.

Maintain an asset register and device allocation history.

Scale supply as M Square closes more societies.

# 5. Business Model

## Initial Commercial Model

Customer: M Square Software Systems Pvt. Ltd.

Contract: B2B annual master agreement, renewable.

Rental: ₹200/device/month (initial assumption).

Typical requirement: 2 devices per society.

Billing: Monthly recurring billing, subject to final commercial agreement.

## Revenue Example

At two devices per society:

Revenue per society = 2 × ₹200 = ₹400/month.

Annual recurring revenue per society = ₹4,800.

20 new societies/month = approximately 40 new devices/month.

30 new societies/month = approximately 60 new devices/month.

Illustrative installed base, assuming all deployments remain active and no churn:

After 12 months at 20 societies/month: 480 devices → ₹96,000/month recurring device revenue.

After 12 months at 30 societies/month: 720 devices → ₹1,44,000/month recurring device revenue.

These figures are revenue illustrations, not profit. Actual profitability depends on procurement price, useful life, repair/replacement, logistics, financing, taxes and other operating costs.

# 16. Sales Objection Handling

Why should we rent instead of buying? Rental converts upfront hardware expenditure into a predictable operating cost and transfers lifecycle management to Techastra.

What happens if a phone fails? Techastra provides a defined repair/replacement SLA under the commercial plan.

What if a device is lost? Loss/theft treatment should be explicitly defined in the MSA and priced through a protection/replacement policy.

Can the phone be locked to our application? Yes, subject to technical feasibility; kiosk/MDM configuration can be offered as an additional managed service.

Can you scale to hundreds or thousands of devices? The model is designed around batch procurement, inventory control and deployment forecasting so capacity can scale with customer acquisition.

# 20. Risks & Mitigation

Low ₹200 margin Validate total cost of ownership and use volume-based procurement.

Device damage/loss Define liability clearly and maintain a replacement reserve/protection plan.

Rapid device obsolescence Standardize a small device portfolio and target 24–36 month lifecycle.

Customer concentration Use M Square as anchor but develop additional PropTech customers.

Inventory risk Procure against confirmed deployment forecasts rather than speculative demand.

Cash-flow pressure Negotiate supplier credit where possible and align customer billing with procurement commitments.

Data/security exposure Use MDM/kiosk controls and clear responsibility boundaries where required.
